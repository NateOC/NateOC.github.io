Laser Engraved Mittens

Overview

As I've gotten more into snowboarding, I've been hoping to switch to mittens. I kept seeing posts and videos of people customizing Kinco mittens, and wanted to do that too. There was one problem though, I'm not particularly artistic, and I didn't have any paint or markers to use on them. I decided to try laser engraving, since I have access to a laser cutter at school, and it would allow me to make very detailed designs without needing to be good at drawing. I ended up with a pretty cool design, and I'm really happy with how they turned out. Once I made my first pair, my friends wanted me to help them engrave their own, and I decided to turn this into a little business: one of my friends goes to school in Colorado, and there's a lot of people there that would want custom mittens. So, after I made mine, I made two more pairs for my friends, and I'm planning on making more in the future as demand increases.

Setting the Mittens Up and Engraving Them

The laser cutter requires completely level surfaces, especially with photo engraving. This was an issue as the mittens weren't flat at all. They bunched up around the wrist and created a ton of folds and wrinkles. I ended up laser cutting a piece of wood that would fit into the mitten and stretch that area flat. I used scrap pieces of wood to prop the glove up flat. The other problem I ran into was that to focus, the laser is pressed up against the surface of the material. This was an issue because the mittens were so soft that the laser would press into them. I ended up using the spectrometer in the engineering room to measure the height of the mittens once they were leveled, and then CNC'd a wax block to that height so the laser could focus properly without pressing into the material.
