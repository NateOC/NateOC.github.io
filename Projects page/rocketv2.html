Model Rockets V2

Overview

This is my third attempt at making a model rocket, using the engine I made myself. I mostly wanted to test the fuel, so I kept the design really simple and small. This time though, I added a guide to the body so I could make use of a launch rod. This made a huge difference. I also used larger fins compared to the body size than I have previously, as stability wasn't exactly a strength of my previous designs.

Launch Pad

After what happened with V1, I knew I needed to have a proper launch rod set up. I 3D printed a launch pad base, laser cut a simple platform to put on top so it didn't melt, and used a 1/8 inch diameter steel rod to guide the rocket in the first part of the launch. This was what I should've had for the V1 rocket, but that was a lesson in skipping steps I had to learn.

Construction

This rocket was really simple in its construction, acting purely as a test of the sorbitol fuel. The entire body was just an engine, with a nose cone on top and fins at the bottom. The fins were really poorly aligned, as I hadn't yet figured out how to properly space them out.

Launch

The quality of this rockets construction was definitely clear in the launch:

I'm definitely going to put iron oxide in the next engine I make, this took off pretty slow. Once it was up it worked great though.

Next Steps

For my next rocket, I'm going to try adding iron oxide to the fuel to increase the burn rate. This should give a lot more thrust, and get the rocket up to a higher altitude. I also need to sort out the fins, and as I make larger rockets, I seriously need to work on a parachute recovery system. This will be a little harder because I'll have to get blackpowder but it will also be a gateway to incorporating electronics, which is going to be fun.
