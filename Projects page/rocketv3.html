Model Rockets V3

Overview

This was my fourth attempt at building a model rocket. I still had an extra E-class engine laying around, and wanted to use it. I also wanted to improve the stability and recovery system of the rocket, so I made several design changes. This ended up being my most successful launch yet, and marks the beginning of more complex rockets in the future.

Fin alignment

To achieve better stability, I decided to 3D print a ring that would space the fins out, and make it easy to attach them completely straight. This helped with stability, made the rocket look better, and made construction easier.

Parachute

With a rocket this big, I couldn't skimp out on the parachute. I still wanted to be as independent as possible, so after a little research I got a nylon shower curtain and cut it into shape before reenforcing the edges with tape. I then connected the parachute to the nose cone, which had loops designed into it for that purpose. The nose cone was also connected to a long shock cord inside the body tube, so when the ejection charge went off, the cone and parachute had some time to decelerate before pulling on the cord. I laser cut one of the center rings with a slightly larger inner diameter, so the shock cord could be attached directly to the engine.

Fin Spacer

This is the spacer I designed, with four slots for each fin. I used two of these to ensure the fins stayed straight and secure.

Improved Fins

I switched to larger, more rectangular fins for improved stability. I put slots in the fins so they would fit onto the spacers snugly as well.

Parachute

This was the complete parachute with the rocket inside it for scale.

Fully Assembled

This was everything put together. I underestimated how much space the parachute would take up in the body, so it was a tight fit, but it all worked out.

Launch

This was the best launch I've had so far. I underestimated the force of the ejection charge and it ended up breaking the shock cord, seperating the nose cone and parachute from the body. However, the rocket flew straight and stable, and reached a good altitude.

I was running really low on wire, so I had to get a little close to launch.

What's next

Now that I've acheived stable flight, I want to focus on improving my own engines and developing a recovery system that incorporates electronics. I'm thinking of using an accelerometer connected to an arduino and a small charge to deploy the parachute at the right time. I would also have to add stages to the body to protect the electronics from the engine and ejection charge. This will be a fun challenge, and I'm excited to get started.
